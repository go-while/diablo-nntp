Problem: a third refused article. Chinese text in talk.politics.china, text/plain; charset=utf-8, Content-Transfer-Encoding: quoted-printable (not base64), ArtType 000b00 on main and with #240. Its 533 body lines look like `=E5=9C=A8=E5=A4=A9...=`: 76 characters of =XX with a soft line break at the end. Those characters are all in the base64 alphabet, and nine consecutive lines of 60 to 77 base64 characters make a base64 binary. This article had 443 such lines (longest run 41) and was flagged at body line 34. Any non-Latin text sent as quoted-printable will do this.

Change (lib/arttype.c): a line with '=' anywhere but in its last two characters is not counted as a base64 line, since base64 uses '=' only as padding at the end of the data. No decoding; one scan of lines that already look like base64.

Tests (tests/e2e/arttype_test.go): quoted-printable Chinese with and without MIME headers (text, not binary; without the change 000b00 and 000a00), and base64 with trailing padding, with and without headers (still binary). The refused article gives 000100 with the change (dfeedtest -a). TestArticleType* pass on Debian (3 of 3).

Questions for review:
- Is '=' before the last two characters the right test? A line like `abcd=` or `ab==` stays base64.
- The same nine-lines rule also fires for uuencode-shaped and BinHex-shaped lines; I have not looked for false positives there.
