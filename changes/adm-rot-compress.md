- `adm/rot` can keep as many old logs as you like and compress them. A new `adm/rot.conf` (read by `rot` and by `daily.atrim`) says how many copies to keep (`keep 14` keeps `file.0` to `file.14`, 15 copies, the same numbering as `rot 14`; 0 to 9999) and how to compress them (`compress gzip`; built in: gzip, bzip2, xz, zstd, compress, or `none`; your own with `method <name> <suffix> <command>`, a filter from standard input to standard output, under a name that is not a built-in one). Yesterday's log (`file.0`) stays plain; `file.1` and older are compressed when `file.1` is made (`general.log.1.gz`, ...). Copies made with an earlier method are rotated with their own suffix until they are dropped. When the count is lowered, `rot` removes the copies above it (`file.<N>`, plain or with a suffix it knows, N above the count), which a larger count left behind; before, they stayed for ever. A compressor that fails leaves the copy plain and `rot` exits 1. `rot -k` checks `rot.conf` and prints the configured count, which `daily.atrim` uses. A `rot.conf` that `rot` cannot read or does not understand (an unknown line or method, a `keep` that is not a number from 0 to 9999), or a `ROT_CONF` naming no file, stops it with exit 2 before any log is touched; without a `rot.conf` nothing changes (4 copies, plain), and neither with the sample `rot.conf` as installed: its `keep 14` and `compress gzip` are commented-out examples.
