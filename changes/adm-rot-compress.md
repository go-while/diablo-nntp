- `adm/rot` can keep as many old logs as you like and compress them. A new `adm/rot.conf` (read by `rot` and by `daily.atrim`) says how many copies to keep (`keep 14` keeps `file.0` to `file.14`, 15 copies, the same numbering as `rot 14`; 0 to 9999) and how to compress them (`compress gzip`; built in: gzip, bzip2, xz, zstd, compress, or `none`; your own with `method <name> <suffix> <command>`, a filter from standard input to standard output, under a name that is not a built-in one). Yesterday's log (`file.0`) stays plain; `file.1` and older are compressed (`general.log.1.gz`, ...); the new `file.1` once every log has been rotated, so that no log waits for the compression of another. Copies made with an earlier method are rotated with their own suffix until they are dropped. When the count is lowered, `rot` removes the copies above it (`file.<N>`, plain or with a suffix it knows, N above the count), which a larger count left behind; before, they stayed for ever. A compressor that fails leaves the copy plain, its messages go to standard error (cron mails them) and `rot` exits 1. The built-in `compress` runs `compress -f -c`, since without `-f` ncompress and BSD compress exit 2 for a log that does not get smaller. `rot -k` checks `rot.conf` and prints the configured count, which `daily.atrim` uses. A `rot.conf` that `rot` cannot read or does not understand (an unknown line or method, a `keep` that is not a number from 0 to 9999), or a `ROT_CONF` naming no file, stops it with exit 2 before any log is touched; without a `rot.conf` nothing changes (4 copies, plain), and neither with the sample `rot.conf` as installed: its `keep 14` and `compress gzip` are commented-out examples.
