- `adm/rot` can keep as many old logs as you like and compress them. A new `adm/rot.conf` (read by `rot` and by `daily.atrim`) says how many copies to keep (`keep 14`) and how to compress them (`compress gzip`; built in: gzip, bzip2, xz, zstd, compress, or `none`; your own with `method <name> <suffix> <command>`, a filter from standard input to standard output). Yesterday's log (`file.0`) stays plain; `file.1` and older are compressed when `file.1` is made (`general.log.1.gz`, ...). Copies made with an earlier method are rotated with their own suffix until they are dropped. A compressor that fails leaves the copy plain and `rot` exits 1. `rot -k` prints the configured count, which `daily.atrim` uses; without a `rot.conf` nothing changes (4 copies, plain). The sample `rot.conf` asks for 14 copies, gzip.
