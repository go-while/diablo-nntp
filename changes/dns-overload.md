- dreaderd: when every DNS worker is busy, dreaderd keeps accepting. A new connection (or the first lookup of a client behind a PROXY or `useproxied` proxy) waits in a short queue while its expected wait is within half of `readerdnswait` (default 5 s), up to `readerdnsqueue` connections (default 256) and three quarters of the descriptor limit; otherwise, or once a new worker time shows it can no longer be served in time, it gets `400 Server too busy` at once. Before, the listener was switched off and a flood waited in the kernel backlog for tens of seconds or got TCP resets. The listen backlog is now as large as the kernel allows (`net.core.somaxconn` on Linux, 4096 by default; it was 256), so a sharp burst does not lose SYNs and wait a TCP retransmit (1 s or more) before it even gets its 400.
- dreaderd: no request waits longer than `readerdnswait` for a DNS worker (it was a fixed 10 s). AUTHINFO logins of connected clients wait ahead of new connections, with no length limit but a descriptor cap, so a login flood no longer answers AUTHINFO PASS with 400; the old limit of `4 * readerdns + 4` queued requests is gone.
- dreaderd: a connection whose descriptor is dropped on the way between the main process and a reader (descriptor limit) is still released; before, it stayed counted until a restart. At startup the soft descriptor limit is raised to the hard limit, at most FD_SETSIZE.
- dreaderd: refusals are logged per reason at most once per 10 seconds, with a count; a reconnect storm past 200 refusals per 100 ms pauses the listener for the rest of the 100 ms; `accept()` running out of descriptors or memory pauses it for a second.
