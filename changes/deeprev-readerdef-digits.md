- dreaderd: a readerdef whose name starts with three digits followed by other characters (`100mbit`) was taken for a refusal from an authentication backend and its name was sent to the client as the greeting; it is now used as a readerdef. A refusal is three digits followed by the end or white space (`502`, `502 Go away`, `502`, TAB, `Go away`, and `502` followed by the CR or LF of a backend answer that ends in a line end), also when a readerdef has that name, so a readerdef name of just three digits (`404`) is not usable. The client gets the code, a space and the text; a refusal without a text gets `NNN Access denied`, and a `600 <minutes>` ban without the code that is to follow it gets `502 Access denied` instead of an empty line. The fields of the `600` form may be separated by TABs too, and a CR or LF ends the refusal text. Name readerdefs with letters, not three digits only.
