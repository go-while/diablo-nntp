- dreaderd: new `readerauthtimeout` (default 10 s, 0 = no limit) bounds each call to an LDAP, NetRemote, Perl or PAM auth backend. Before, a backend that did not answer held a DNS worker for good (LDAP, Perl, PAM, the NetRemote read) or for about two minutes per address (the NetRemote connect), and logins stopped once all `readerdns` workers were held. A login that runs out of time is refused and logged, and no further dreader.access line is tried for it. RADIUS keeps its own timeout and tries.
- dreaderd: LDAP and NetRemote share the limit out over the steps of their exchange as their own timeouts (LDAP: network and operation timeouts, a bind and a search with the time left; NetRemote: a non-blocking connect, reads and writes with one deadline, a getaddrinfo lookup), and the worker goes on. Perl and PAM, or a call still running 2 s past the limit, are cut off by the worker's alarm: the worker sends the refusal and exits, and dreaderd starts a new one. Also: NetRemote builds with OpenSSL 1.1 and later again, the old LDAP code closes its connection after a failed bind (it leaked a descriptor per login), and in builds without INET6 an ident lookup ends at its timeout in all.
- dreaderd: installing a changed dreader.access renames the five cache files under an exclusive lock, so a process loading the cache gets all old or all new files; before, both sides took a shared lock and a reader could load a mix. While a reader holds the lock, the renames wait for the next pass of the main loop; a wait of more than 10 s is logged, then once a minute.
