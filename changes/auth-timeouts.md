- dreaderd: new `readerauthtimeout` (default 10 s, 0 = no limit) bounds each call to an LDAP, NetRemote, Perl or PAM auth backend. Before, a backend that did not answer held a DNS worker for good (LDAP, Perl, PAM, the NetRemote read) or for about two minutes per address (the NetRemote connect), and logins stopped once all `readerdns` workers were held. A login that runs out of time is refused and logged, and the worker serves the next one. RADIUS keeps its own timeout and tries.
- dreaderd: LDAP sets its network and operation timeouts and binds and searches with a time limit; NetRemote connects without blocking and reads with a deadline (also its session-exit accounting); a Perl `checkuser` dies at the limit, and PAM or a call still running 2 s later is cut off by the worker's alarm. NetRemote builds against OpenSSL 1.1 and later again, and the old LDAP code closes its connection after a failed bind (it leaked a descriptor per login). In builds without INET6, an ident lookup ends at its timeout in all, no longer up to 10 s more per read.
- dreaderd: installing a changed dreader.access renames the five cache files under an exclusive lock, so a process loading the cache gets all old or all new files; before, both sides took a shared lock and a reader could load a mix. While a reader holds the lock, the renames wait for the next pass of the main loop.
