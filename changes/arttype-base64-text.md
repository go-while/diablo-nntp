- A text part sent as base64 (`Content-Type: text/...`, or no Content-Type, with `Content-Transfer-Encoding: base64`) is no longer taken for a binary because nine or more of its lines look like base64. Mail gateways (the linux.* hierarchy) and news clients send non-ASCII text this way, and such articles were refused as a binary in non-binary groups (`IncomingFilter`, ArtType 000b00). `lib/arttype.c` now decodes the body of such a part, at the end of its header block, for the article or for each MIME part up to the next `--` line. The part is a binary when a decoded byte is NUL, DEL or a control character other than TAB, LF, FF, CR and ESC, and the decoded lines get the same checks as an unencoded body (the yEnc header line, and runs of uuencode, BinHex and base64 lines), so base64 of a yEnc, uuencoded or base64 file is still a binary, and a clear-signed text is not. Decoding carries on across lines, so the line width of the encoding does not change the result. White space and other characters that decoders skip are skipped, and data after `=` padding is decoded too. A line that starts with `--` (a MIME boundary, a signature separator) ends the decoding. A last decoded line without a newline gets the checks too, at that line or at the end of the article (`ArticleTypeEnd()`, called by `diablo` at the `.` line and by `dfeedtest -a` at the end of its input). A part with no Content-Type counts as text/plain, as RFC 2045 says; `B64_NOCTYPE_IS_TEXT` in `lib/arttype.c` turns that off. `diablo` and `dfeedtest -a` now hand the classifier a piece of a long line (no newline yet) whole: they used to drop its last character. Text in UTF-16 or UTF-32 has NULs and still counts as a binary. Decoding costs about 5 ns per byte of a base64 text body: a binary stops at its first non-text byte.
