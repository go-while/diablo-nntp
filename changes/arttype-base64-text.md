- A text part sent as base64 (`Content-Type: text/...`, or no Content-Type, with `Content-Transfer-Encoding: base64`) is no longer taken for a binary because nine or more of its lines look like base64. Mail gateways (the linux.* hierarchy) and news clients send non-ASCII text this way, and such articles were refused as a binary in non-binary groups (`IncomingFilter`, ArtType 000b00). `lib/arttype.c` now decodes the body of such a part. The part is a binary when a decoded byte is NUL, DEL or a control character other than TAB, LF, FF, CR and ESC, and the decoded lines (ended by LF, CR or CRLF) get the same checks as an unencoded body (the yEnc header line, and runs of uuencode, BinHex and base64 lines), so base64 of a yEnc, uuencoded or base64 file is still a binary, and a clear-signed text is not. Decoding carries on across lines, so the line width of the encoding does not change the result. White space and other characters that decoders skip are skipped, and data after `=` padding is decoded too. A `-- ` signature separator ends the decoding: the signature after it is checked as it is. A last decoded line without a newline gets the checks too, at the end of its part or of the article (`ArticleTypeEnd()`, called by `diablo` at the `.` line and by `dfeedtest -a` at the end of its input). A part with no Content-Type counts as text/plain, as RFC 2045 says; `B64_NOCTYPE_IS_TEXT` in `lib/arttype.c` turns that off.
- Only a part's header block says how it is encoded: the article's headers, or the lines after a delimiter line of a multipart we are in up to the next blank line. The boundary is the `boundary` parameter of a `multipart/` Content-Type, read as a MIME parameter (quoted strings and comments skipped, so `name="x boundary=y"` is not one; white space and folds around `=` and inside a quoted value; the name in any case; quoted pairs; up to 70 characters after unquoting); a boundary on another type is ignored. A delimiter is `--boundary`, or `--boundary--` to close, with only white space after it. A delimiter ends its part and all it said (type, encoding, decoding); a closing one ends the multipart and any open inside it, and also a header block it interrupts. Multiparts nested more than four deep are not followed, and their parts are not decoded. A `Content-Transfer-Encoding` line in a body, or a `--` line that is no delimiter, changes nothing.
- `diablo` and `dfeedtest -a` now hand the classifier a piece of a long line (no newline yet) whole: they used to drop its last character. `dfeedtest -a` takes a CRLF blank line for the end of the headers. Text in UTF-16 or UTF-32 has NULs and still counts as a binary, and 8-bit data without control bytes counts as text (it cannot be told from Latin-1 text). Decoding costs about 5 ns per byte of a base64 text body: a binary stops at its first non-text byte.
