- dreaderd: the main process no longer waits for room in a reader's socket. A client on its way to a reader that has no room is queued, with its descriptor, and sent when the reader reads again. Before, a burst of logins could fill both directions between the main process and a reader; each then waited in SendMsg() for the other to read, and dreaderd stopped serving (logins hung, new clients got no greeting) until it was restarted. It showed on illumos, where the socketpairs are SOCK_STREAM (no SOCK_SEQPACKET) with about 16 KiB per direction: 40 AUTHINFO logins at once were enough. With SOCK_SEQPACKET and 256 KiB buffers (Linux, FreeBSD) it takes a much larger burst. New clients go to a reader with no queue when there is one, and a queued client waits at most readerdnswait seconds before it gets "400 Server too busy".
- dreaderd: a reader or DNS worker forked by the main process no longer inherits the client descriptors the main process holds for later (queued for a reader or a DNS worker, or waiting for a delayed close). Such a copy kept the client's connection open after its session ended: the client got 205 and never saw the connection close.
